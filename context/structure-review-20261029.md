# Revised article structure for Mario's approval

**Working title:** *The Research Map: A Method and Human-Supervised AI System for Structuring Research Plans*. Target journal: *Information Processing & Management*. Authors (order updated 30 October 2026): Sérgio Luiz Braga França, Escola de Engenharia/UFF; Mario Reis, Instituto de Física/UFF. This file is the editable structural decision record; the LaTeX outline mirrors it. Section 2 is already drafted and remains unchanged.

**Descriptive question for this article:** How are the Research Map's methodological relations represented, supported, and limited in a human-supervised AI application? The word *efficiency* is absent from the working title because this article has no time, delay, or outcome measurement.

## How Sergio's new map enters the article

The DOCX exported from the application contains (a) Sergio's original answers in its appendix and (b) a generated proposal for a future mixed-methods intervention study. These have different evidentiary roles. The appendix is an author-supplied account of the motivation: difficulty narrowing themes, formulating a problem and objectives, and outlining subsequent chapters. The generated proposal is an illustration of what the app suggested, not evidence that delays, low quality, dropout, or efficiency effects were measured. Its literature codes R01–R10 are leads for later verification, not ready-to-cite support.

| Decision | Content from the DOCX | Place in this article |
|---|---|---|
| Retain and refine | Initial planning is difficult when theme, problem, objectives, and chapter topics are not connected. | Introduction, framed as Sergio's motivating observation and a design problem; external prevalence and causal claims require independent evidence. |
| Retain and refine | A simplified, coherent planning method is proposed. | Method section, grounded in Sergio's primary August documents and explained through five stages plus the objective–method matrix. |
| Translate cautiously | The generated map proposes identifying obstacles and comparing tools. | State of the art and discussion as rationale and comparison, without claiming a systematic mapping or diagnosis of students. |
| Defer as future research | Questionnaires, interviews, workshops, pre/post rubrics, delays, time savings, adoption, and effectiveness. | Discussion/Future work only, explicitly unperformed; no results, sample, CEP approval, or effect estimate in this article. |
| Exclude from evidence | Generated expected results, duplicated warning messages, broad impact promises, and R01–R10 citations not checked against the claims. | Do not import as findings or references in the manuscript. |

## Proposed outline and function of each part

1. **Introduction**
   - **1.1 Planning problem and motivation.** Present Sergio's observation about the difficulty of connecting a research theme, central problem, objectives, and chapter topics. Distinguish this design motivation from an empirical estimate of student difficulties.
   - **1.2 Aim and contribution.** State the descriptive question and aim: explain the Research Map method and examine how its relations are operationalized in the human-supervised application and agent.
   - **1.3 Evidence and scope.** Identify the primary methodological documents, inspected implementation, focused literature, and constructed illustration. State that no user outcomes or efficiency effects are reported.
   - **1.4 Article organization.** Preview the method → system → illustrative path → limits sequence.
2. **State of the art.** Retain the completed comparison of scholarly work, standards, and documented applications; it now explains which capabilities predate the Research Map and where the specific method-to-system account sits.
3. **The Research Map method**
   - **3.1 Origin, sources, and purpose.** Present the authorial source, the 3 and 7 August method documents, and the problem the method is designed to address.
   - **3.2 Five stages and their relations.** Explain problem, general objective, specific objectives, literature topics, and development/analysis topics. Research questions are a validation concern in the detailed method, not a sixth stage.
   - **3.3 Objective–method matrix.** For each specific objective: planned collection, analysis/treatment, and expected result or impact; distinguish plan from observed finding.
   - **3.4 Revision and human judgment.** Explain alignment and dependency recommendations, points of author choice, and what the method does not formally establish. Include the interpretive method diagram.
4. **Operationalization in the application and AI agent**
   - **4.1 Method-to-system correspondence.** A compact table maps each method component to implemented, partial, or prospective behavior.
   - **4.2 Information model and guided proposals.** Describe versions, draft and approved elements, references, links, and AI suggestions.
   - **4.3 Checks, decisions, and revision.** Separate deterministic/lexical checks from methodological judgment; explain human confirmation and conservative type-level stale marking.
   - **4.4 Final review and export.** Explain advisory coherence review and conditional PDF/DOCX generation without claiming a formal guarantee. Include architecture and revision diagrams.
5. **Constructed illustrative walkthrough**
   - **5.1 Initial synthetic plan.** Use the already prepared fictional library case, clearly labelled as constructed.
   - **5.2 One upstream change.** Show how a revised central problem prompts review of objectives, topics, and matrix entries.
   - **5.3 What the illustration demonstrates.** Distinguish the conceptual path from verified source-code behavior and from any actual session or user result.
6. **Discussion and limitations**
   - **6.1 Contribution and closest comparators.** Discuss the specific methodological mapping against the papers and products in Section 2, without a priority claim.
   - **6.2 Implementation and evidentiary limits.** Include missing dedicated research-question unit, type-level invalidation, advisory final review, and bibliographic provenance limits.
   - **6.3 Future evaluation.** Place Sergio's proposed interviews, workshops, rubrics, delays, and efficiency measures here as possible future studies, not work already performed.
7. **Conclusion.** Summarize only the documented method, implemented system, illustrative path, and limits.

**Back matter:** declarations, verified references, and possible supplementary primary method documents after coauthor and journal review. No new literature from R01–R10 has been inserted automatically.
